Automatically identifying struggling students learning to program is essential for providing timely and personalized support. While several AI-based methods have been proposed to predict such struggle, they often fail to explicitly model the student's underlying programming skills. This work introduces the Proficiency Taxonomy Model (PTM), a novel approach that incorporates a structured taxonomy of coding proficiencies, developed in collaboration with educators, into the prediction process. The taxonomy captures how students typically approach and solve coding tasks, and is embedded directly within the model. PTM simultaneously learns representations of student skill levels from their coding history and predicts whether they will struggle with future tasks. We evaluated our model on two datasets from introductory Java and Python programming courses for beginners. Our results show that PTM significantly outperforms several state-of-the-art baselines in predicting student struggle. This study highlights the value of combining structured educational insight with deep learning models to support early and accurate identification of students in need.
