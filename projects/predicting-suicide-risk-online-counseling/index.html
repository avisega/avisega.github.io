Online counseling services handle thousands of mental health crises each year, relying on counselors to identify suicide risk during text-based conversations. In collaboration with the National Science Foundation, his project introduces SR-BERT, a hierarchical BERT model that reasons about conversation structure and embeds psychological knowledge to detect suicide risk in real time. SR-BERT encodes each message separately, capturing turn-taking and dialogue context without truncation, and is pre-trained using a Suicide Risk Factors (SRF) lexicon derived from clinical theory. Tested on over 40,000 Hebrew chat sessions from Israel’s national hotline (Sahar), the model significantly outperformed prior non-hierarchical approaches, achieving superior recall and F2 scores - critical metrics in suicide prevention. Importantly, SR-BERT maintained high performance even at early conversation stages, demonstrating its potential for real-time alerting and counselor support in online crisis settings.
