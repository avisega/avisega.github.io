Aligning LLMs for Sensitive Conversational AI

Developing Hebrew and Arabic domain-aligned language models with privacy-preserving fine-tuning

2025, In Progress · AI & LLM Engineering · Co-PI

This project, conducted in cooperation with the National Innovation Authority, develops advanced Hebrew and Arabic domain-aligned language models for sensitive, high-stakes conversational AI. The work focuses on secure fine-tuning and alignment of transformer-based architectures using rigorously de-identified real-world dialogue data, integrating reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and privacy-preserving optimization. Beyond improving model robustness and cultural alignment, the project explores few-shot and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods to enhance contextual reasoning in low-resource languages. By combining deep technical innovation with ethical safeguards, this initiative establishes new standards for trustworthy LLM deployment in domains requiring empathy, discretion, and reliability.

Adaptive Agentic System for Personalized Skill Development

Integrating LLM-based simulation, RAG, and reinforcement learning for dynamic expertise modeling

2025, In Progress · AI & Adaptive Systems · Co-PI

Supported by the Ministry of Education, this project develops Rambam, an adaptive agentic system that models user expertise and dynamically orchestrates personalized skill-growth trajectories. The system integrates retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for context-sensitive knowledge retrieval, few-shot and fine-tuned LLM components for domain adaptation, and reinforcement learning (RL) to optimize sequencing and feedback policies over time. A simulation layer powered by generative agents enables realistic, data-driven interaction scenarios that support behavioral modeling and autonomous evaluation of user strategies. Rambam’s architecture combines structured knowledge representation, real-time inference, and policy optimization to deliver a scalable framework for human-AI co-learning, continuous assessment, and adaptive performance enhancement across professional and operational domains.

Detecting Struggling Student Programmers using Proficiency Taxonomies

Early-warning AI that models programming skills to flag at-risk students

2025 · AI & LLM · Co-PI

Early identification of struggling programmers enables targeted support at scale. The project introduces the Proficiency Taxonomy Model (PTM), which embeds a proficiency taxonomy co-designed with educators directly into the prediction process and learns from each student’s full submission history. PTM builds a Taxonomy-Based Proficiency Profile across 13 dimensions—ten observable programming concepts and three latent skills—producing interpretable skill scores that map to concrete interventions. PTM then combines this profile with the target task’s text and required concepts through a cross-attention module to forecast struggle on upcoming tasks. Evaluations on two real course datasets - CodeWorkout (introductory Java) and FalconCode (introductory Python) - showed that PTM outperformed strong baselines and generalized across languages. Analyses included ablations isolating the value of the taxonomy and of long submission histories, sensitivity studies on sequence length, and statistical significance and calibration checks across multiple metrics. Together, these results position PTM as a practical early-warning capability that is interpretable, data-efficient, and directly aligned with instructional goals.

Dual-Mode Explanations for Suicide Risk Detection in Online Mental Health Support

Framework combining extractive and abstractive layers for transparent, clinically grounded explanations

2025 · AI & Mental Health · PI/Lead

Mental health support services increasingly use AI systems to detect suicide risk in chat conversations, yet clinicians often struggle to trust predictions due to opaque reasoning. Developed with support from the National Science Foundation, this project introduces a dual-mode explanation framework that bridges model outputs with clinical reasoning. The extractive layer highlights help-seeker utterances most responsible for predictions using BCombined, an ensemble of SHAP, LIME, Integrated Gradients, and Embedding-shift relevance. The abstractive layer maps these utterances to psychological risk categories from a curated Suicide Risk Factors lexicon with the aid of large language models. Evaluation on 30,000+ Hebrew hotline sessions and an English Reddit dataset shows cross-language applicability, while a user study with counselors at Israel’s national hotline (Sahar) demonstrated improved helpfulness and understandability without loss of trust. This work advances explainable AI in high-stakes clinical settings, providing transparent, theory-grounded explanations that enhance decision-making while preserving human agency.

QBlooms: Teacher-in-the-Loop MCQ Generation Aligned with Bloom’s Taxonomy

Pedagogy-grounded LLM workflow that turns course content and instructor feedback into higher-order questions

2024 · AI & LLM · PI/Lead

QBlooms addresses the bottleneck of authoring high-quality, higher-order questions by encoding Bloom’s levels as actionable rubrics, using an LLM to draft items (stem, key, distractors, rationale), and iteratively refining them with structured instructor feedback. The project emphasizes interpretability (explicit cognitive targets and misconception-aware distractors) and repeatability (a rubric-driven workflow instructors can reuse across topics). In a university course deployment, expert reviews across multiple rounds showed improvements on key evaluation dimensions—including overall item quality, alignment with intended Bloom levels, and instructor willingness to adopt—demonstrating that a teacher-in-the-loop process can raise question quality without sacrificing rigor or control. This positions QBlooms as a practical path to scalable, standards-aligned assessment design for real deployments, backed by structured evaluations and transparent criteria.

Transparency-Check: Designing and Auditing Transparency in AI-Based Personalization Systems

A European collaboration bridging ethical theory, regulation, and AI design

2024 · AI & Ethics · Partner

Transparency-Check is a practical framework developed under the European Horizon 2020 WeNet - The Internet of Us collaboration to make AI personalization systems more transparent, ethical, and user-centric. The project bridges ethical theory, regulation, and AI design by introducing a structured checklist that evaluates transparency across data input, algorithmic processing, output presentation, and user control. Applied to global platforms such as Amazon, Netflix, and Spotify, the framework exposes gaps in user trust and comprehension, translating them into actionable design principles for responsible AI development. Transparency-Check provides companies, researchers, and policymakers with a scalable instrument for auditing and improving algorithmic transparency—an essential capability in an era of regulatory scrutiny and growing demand for explainable, trustworthy AI.

Predicting Suicide Risk in Online Counseling Services

Hierarchical BERT model integrating conversation structure and psychological theory for real-time risk detection

2023 · AI & Mental Health · PI/Lead

Online counseling services handle thousands of mental health crises each year, relying on counselors to identify suicide risk during text-based conversations. In collaboration with the National Science Foundation, his project introduces SR-BERT, a hierarchical BERT model that reasons about conversation structure and embeds psychological knowledge to detect suicide risk in real time. SR-BERT encodes each message separately, capturing turn-taking and dialogue context without truncation, and is pre-trained using a Suicide Risk Factors (SRF) lexicon derived from clinical theory. Tested on over 40,000 Hebrew chat sessions from Israel’s national hotline (Sahar), the model significantly outperformed prior non-hierarchical approaches, achieving superior recall and F2 scores - critical metrics in suicide prevention. Importantly, SR-BERT maintained high performance even at early conversation stages, demonstrating its potential for real-time alerting and counselor support in online crisis settings.

Predicting Bug Fix Time in Students’ Programming with Deep Language Models

Modeling novice programmers’ code evolution to identify struggling students

2023 · AI & Education · PI/Lead

Automatically identifying struggling students can help instructors provide timely, targeted support. This project presents a deep-learning approach for predicting bug-fix time: the duration between when a student introduces a bug and when it is fixed. The model integrates a transformer-based code embedding (CodeBERT) with a time-aware LSTM that captures how each student’s code evolves over multiple snapshots, combined with contextual meta-features such as compilation history and experience level. Using data from over 300,000 Java code submissions in two educational environments (BlueJ and CodeWorkout), the model outperformed baseline methods based on Halstead metrics, Code2Vec embeddings, and single-snapshot variants. Results show that incorporating multiple code snapshots and temporal dynamics significantly improves the prediction of long bug-fix times, enabling early detection of struggling learners and supporting intelligent tutoring applications.

Optimizing Representations and Policies for Information Sequencing with Reinforcement Learning

Offline optimization of state representations and sequencing policies using large-scale behavioral data

2022 · AI & Reinforcement Learning · PI/Lead

This project develops a reinforcement learning framework for optimizing both the representation of user interaction data and the policies that govern information sequencing. Using large-scale behavioral traces, the system derives compact yet expressive state representations through a greedy feature augmentation process and learns policies that adapt dynamically to user behavior under uncertainty. The research introduces a full offline pipeline combining model-based policy iteration, importance sampling, and Monte Carlo evaluation to ensure stability and robustness across different environment dynamics. The findings show that smaller, well-structured representations can outperform complex ones, revealing principles for efficient policy design in sequential decision systems where interaction data is limited or costly to collect.

Human–AI Collaboration in Citizen Science

A new workflow with Microsoft Research and the University of Oxford to enhance volunteer engagement through hybrid intelligence

2022 · AI & Human Collaboration · Co-PI

This project, developed jointly with Microsoft Research and the University of Oxford, rethinks how humans and AI can collaborate in large-scale citizen science platforms. It introduces a hybrid workflow in which AI models assist volunteers by classifying easy tasks automatically while routing more complex cases to human participants. The system also adapts motivational messages to sustain engagement and productivity without compromising autonomy or data quality. Deployed in Galaxy Zoo - the world’s largest citizen science astronomy project - the approach demonstrated that human-AI cooperation can increase throughput and retention while maintaining scientific accuracy. The project exemplifies how human-centered AI design and behavioral modeling can scale collective intelligence in real-world digital ecosystems.

Difficulty Ranking for Personalized Information Sequencing

Collaborative filtering and social choice methods for ranking under uncertainty

2019 · AI & Personalization · PI/Lead

This project introduces a ranking-based framework for personalizing information sequencing using collaborative filtering and social choice theory. Instead of predicting user performance or preferences directly, the system constructs individualized difficulty rankings by aggregating multiple behavioral signals—accuracy, retries, and response time—across similar users. The approach, called EduRank, combines ranking aggregation from recommender systems with weighted voting mechanisms from computational social choice, enabling fine-grained personalization without relying on absolute scores. Tested on large-scale behavioral datasets, the method outperformed standard ranking and matrix factorization approaches, showing how ordinal data and user similarity can be leveraged to optimize personalized content ordering in any domain where difficulty or complexity must be adapted dynamically to the individual.

Trajectory-Corrected Intervention: Optimizing Engagement Policies with Offline Reinforcement Learning

Developed with Microsoft Research and the University of Oxford

2018 · AI & Reinforcement Learning · Co-PI

This project introduces Trajectory-Corrected Intervention (TCI), a reinforcement learning framework for optimizing engagement policies in large-scale human-AI systems. TCI learns when and how to intervene by balancing short-term disruption costs with long-term behavioral outcomes. The method combines model-based policy optimization with offline evaluation, iteratively refining both the representation of user trajectories and the resulting decision policy without relying on online experimentation. Developed jointly with Microsoft Research and the University of Oxford, TCI was deployed in a live online platform and demonstrated significant improvements in sustained engagement and contribution. The project highlights how hybrid AI systems can safely and effectively optimize interventions in dynamic, human-centered environments.

Real-Time Human–AI Monitoring for Group Interaction Analysis

Combining NLP and visualization for intelligent oversight and intervention

2017 · AI & Human Collaboration · PI/Lead

This project developed a real-time AI system for monitoring multi-user interactions and identifying critical moments that require human attention. The framework integrates shallow natural language processing with visualization tools to detect and classify conversational states such as consensus formation, inactivity, or technical challenges. These are surfaced to a human operator through an adaptive alert dashboard, enabling effective oversight across multiple concurrent sessions. The system architecture combines message-level classification, temporal pattern detection, and event visualization, supporting scalable human-AI collaboration in complex communication environments. Evaluations in live settings demonstrated that the system achieved expert-level recognition accuracy while improving the efficiency and quality of human interventions.

Environment Change Detection and Vision-Based Interaction System

Patent: System and Method for Detecting Changes in an Environment

2006 · AI & Computer Vision · Co-Inventor

This patented system introduces an early vision-based framework for detecting and interpreting changes within a physical environment using a single camera and display pair. The method compares captured images against known reference frames to identify motion, object presence, and shape changes without relying on external sensors or markers. By dynamically modeling background and lighting conditions, the system extracts silhouettes and movement trajectories in real time, enabling seamless interaction with projected or displayed visual content. Originally designed for interactive public installations and advanced motion interfaces, this work anticipated key principles later used in immersive displays, gesture control, and adaptive vision systems.
